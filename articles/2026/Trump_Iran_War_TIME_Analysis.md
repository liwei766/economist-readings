# Inside Trump's Search for a Way Out of the Iran War

> **作者：** Eric Cortellessa

## 一、英文原文 (Original English Text)

Donald Trump was in the Oval Office during the third week of the Iran war when a group of his most trusted advisers came to deliver some unwelcome news. His longtime pollster, Tony Fabrizio, had conducted surveys that indicated the war Trump launched was growing increasingly unpopular. Gas prices had surged past $4 per gallon, stock markets had tumbled to multi-year lows, and millions of Americans were preparing to take to the streets in protest. Thirteen American service members had been confirmed killed. Some of Trump's key public supporters were criticizing a conflict with no clear end in sight. It fell on White House chief of staff Susie Wiles and a small group of aides to tell the President that the longer the war dragged on, the more it would threaten his public support and Republicans' prospects in November's midterm elections.

For Trump, the stark warning was unsettling. The President has begun many recent mornings watching video clips compiled by military officials of battlefield successes, according to a senior Administration official. He has told advisers that being the commander in chief to eliminate the nuclear threat posed by Iran could be one of his signature achievements. But Wiles, according to two White House sources, was concerned aides were giving the President a rose-colored view of how the war was being perceived domestically, telling Trump what he wanted to hear instead of what he needed to hear. She had urged colleagues, the officials say, to be "more forthright with the boss" about the political and economic risks.

The meeting reflected a reality the White House can no longer ignore: time is running out before the President, his party, and the American public pay an even steeper price. Trump had promised to revive the economy and keep the U.S. out of foreign conflicts. Now he has started a war he had not gotten a mandate to wage, and the economic pain may only be beginning. A month into the largest oil shock in modern history, global growth forecasts are being slashed, shortages are emerging across Europe and Asia, and energy traders warn the world has yet to feel the full severity of the disruption. A prolonged closure of the Strait of Hormuz, the narrow waterway that is the primary exit route for oil and gas from the Persian Gulf, could tip the global economy into recession.

The President was left frustrated by the predicament, at odds with some of his own officials, and fuming at the negative impressions of the war. The mounting political and economic toll has left him looking for an off-ramp, according to two advisers and two members of Congress who have spoken to him during the last week. Trump told them he wants to wind down the campaign, wary of a protracted conflict that could hobble Republicans heading into the midterms. At the same time, he wants the operation to be a decisive success. Allies say he is searching for a way to declare victory, halt the fighting, and hope that economic conditions stabilize before the political damage hardens. "There's a narrow window," says a senior Administration official, who like others interviewed for this account of Trump at war was granted anonymity to provide candid observations about the President's thinking.

Trump tried to thread the needle in a primetime address to the nation on April 1. He trumpeted the military triumphs and said the operation was "nearing its completion," while also saying the U.S. would strike the Islamic Republic "extremely hard" over the next two to three weeks, threatening to eviscerate the nation's energy infrastructure. "We're going to bring them back to the stone ages," the President said, "where they belong."

In a phone interview the next morning, Trump told TIME that Iran was eager to make a deal to end the fighting. "Why wouldn't they call? We just blew up their three big bridges last night," the President says. "They're getting decimated. They say Trump is not negotiating with Iran. I mean, it's sort of an easy negotiation."

And yet behind the bluster has been a growing recognition within the West Wing that the situation may be slipping out of its control. Key Trump officials, including Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, were surprised by the barrage of retaliatory attacks Tehran launched against U.S. and Israeli targets across the region, including in countries long assumed to be off-limits: Kuwait, Bahrain, Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates, and Qatar, a state that had both harbored Iran's terrorist proxies and served as a conduit for backchannel diplomacy between the U.S. and Hamas. The response shattered the assumption that Tehran would confine itself to performative retaliation. In internal deliberations before the war's launch, Hegseth had pointed to Iran's muted reaction to Trump's past attacks as evidence that calibrated force could impose costs on Tehran without triggering a broader war. Hegseth "was caught off guard. There's no question," says a person familiar with his thinking.

The Pentagon disputes the account. "The U.S. military is the most advanced, comprehensive, and battle-tested planning organization in the world. Long before Operation Epic Fury launched, we had already anticipated, war-gamed, and fully prepared for every possible Iranian response, from the weakest possible reaction to the most extreme escalation," Hegseth's chief spokesman Sean Parnell tells TIME. "Nothing Iran does surprises us. We are ready, we are dominant, and we are winning."

By the Pentagon's accounting, Operation Epic Fury has been an unambiguous military success, leaving 90% of Iran's missile capacity degraded or destroyed, roughly 70% of its launchers neutralized, more than 150 naval vessels disabled or destroyed, and Iranian Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei killed, along with many of his top lieutenants. Yet it seems increasingly unlikely Trump will achieve the broader objectives he trumpeted—permanently blocking Tehran's path to a nuclear weapon, dismantling its ballistic missile program, and replacing the Islamic Republic's theocratic hardliners with a friendlier regime—on the compressed timeline the White House has embraced.

In his speech, Trump cast the operation as on the cusp of victory. "We have all the cards. They have none," he said. "We are on track to complete all of America's military objectives shortly." But the endgame remains murky: Trump simultaneously pledged to ramp up the fighting and wind it down. He has vowed to use unprecedented means to unleash devastating force against Iran, but tells TIME that he would never allow artificial intelligence to make lethal decisions, insisting that a human would always control the chain of command. "I wouldn't allow AI to do it," Trump says. "I respect AI. It's a decision that a president has to make—assuming he's competent." Beyond that, there are very few options he appears willing to take off the table.

Steve Witkoff, the President's longtime friend and envoy, frames this as the product of a business career in which maintaining optionality was paramount. "Donald Trump always has multiple exit strategies," Witkoff has told colleagues at the White House and State Department. "He keeps a lot of options, a lot of off-ramps, and then feels his way through the process." But wars have a way of outrunning a President's plans. The risk of Trump's gamble is that intensifying the military campaign in the coming weeks will do more to close exit routes than to create them.

As preparations for the war began, the Administration believed it had a winning formula. The U.S. would deliver an opening strike so overwhelming Tehran's only viable response would be limited retaliation—enough to satisfy domestic audiences without inviting more attacks. It was a theory rooted in precedent. When Trump ordered the killing of Iranian general Qasem Soleimani during his first term, Iran's response was a missile strike on a U.S. base that caused no casualties and was telegraphed in advance. After Operation Midnight Hammer, the June 2025 air campaign against Iran's nuclear facilities, the retaliation was similarly tempered.

Trump has long favored what aides call "one-and-done" operations. He has launched them in Yemen, Syria, and Somalia. In January, he pulled off the audacious capture of Venezuelan leader Nicolás Maduro, spiriting the autocrat out of the country to face trial in the U.S., and creating room for the ascension of a more compliant partner, acting president Delcy Rodriguez. He then moved to facilitate U.S. access to Venezuela's oil reserves, among the largest in the world. Aides say Trump saw Venezuela as a demonstration that a swift, surgical intervention could topple a hostile regime, install a cooperative replacement, and secure American interests without drawing the nation into an open-ended confrontation.

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, a champion of military aggression against Iran, had a different idea of how things might go. Over the last six months, Netanyahu repeatedly told Trump that the past successes against Iran should serve as a prelude for a more sustained, final campaign, an Israeli official tells TIME. On Feb. 11, Netanyahu came to Washington for a private meeting with the President that stretched for hours. "We've come this far, Donald," Netanyahu told Trump, according to a source present. "We have to finish what we started." Iran was playing for time, Netanyahu told Trump, and would race toward a bomb in secret. "After they got hit the last time, they thought they had nothing to lose," says another Israeli official, arguing that Tehran would see the development of nuclear weapons as the only way to prevent such an onslaught from happening again.

The plan of attack was set in motion nearly a month before it was executed, according to two senior U.S. officials. It took weeks of meticulous coordination, much of it conducted in close consultation with Israeli counterparts. When the New York Times published details of the planning of the operation on Feb. 17, Trump exploded at aides, unleashing a string of profanities, according to a senior Administration official. The President then told reporters he would decide on strikes within "10, 15 days," although he knew the U.S. was planning to attack much sooner. "He was intentionally engaged in public misdirection to protect the mission," a White House official says.

Trump became wary enough of leaks that some of his own aides were the target of subterfuge. On Feb. 27, he traveled to Mar-a-Lago. Aides assembled in a makeshift Situation Room. Trump bristled at the number of people present. "He thought the group was too big," one official recalls; it included people Trump didn't recognize or didn't feel he knew well enough. At one point, the President snapped that the operation was off. He said he would keep deliberating. This was another head fake: Trump had already made up his mind to attack that very night. Once the room cleared, he called back a smaller, trusted circle—those he wanted beside him as the first bombs fell.

That evening, Trump had dinner on the patio of Mar-a-Lago with a group that included deputy chief of staff Stephen Miller, Secretary of State Marco Rubio, Witkoff, and White House counsel David Warrington. Not present was Vice President J.D. Vance, who was in the Situation Room back in Washington. A Trump official says that was a reflection of standard continuity-of-government protocol, which calls for the President and Vice President to be kept apart during sensitive national security operations when both are not at the White House. Of the President's inner circle, Vance had pushed hardest against the operation, according to two sources familiar with the deliberations. "J.D. really doesn't like this," Trump told the group gathered under the Palm Beach stars. "But when the decision is made, it's a decision, right?"

A White House source says that Vance, in the lead-up to the offensive, laid out what he saw as both the benefits and the risks, adding that "once the President makes the decision, the Vice President stands by him 110%." (A Vance aide declined to comment.)

Operation Epic Fury began with a sweeping round of strikes that killed Iran's supreme leader. Tehran's response was expansive: volleys of missiles and drones targeting U.S. bases in Iraq and Syria, barrages against Israeli cities, harassment of commercial shipping in the Persian Gulf, and coordinated attacks by proxy militias across the region. Hegseth was among those taken aback, says the person familiar with his thinking: "He was expecting the Iranians to fight back in some form. When they started attacking virtually the entire region, it sort of hit him like, 'Whoa, we're really in this now.'"

The Administration also appeared to be taken by surprise when Iran reached for a source of leverage: control over the Strait of Hormuz, which roughly 20% of the world's oil supply passes through each day. In response to U.S. strikes, Tehran implemented a de facto blockade, declaring the channel effectively closed and restricting passage to non-hostile vessels. The resulting economic shock had domestic reverberations that went beyond the expectations of Trump's inner circle. As gas prices skyrocketed, Trump sought to recast the higher costs as a necessary trade-off—a short-term burden in service of eliminating the threat of a nuclear-armed Iran.

On one hand, Trump saw value in Iran's aggressive response, believing it would validate his case that the Islamic Republic posed a destabilizing threat. "Now that you've seen how they behaved over the last two days against other countries in the region, they would have wiped them all out," Trump told me during a March 4 phone call. Yet advisers were concerned the war would alienate political supporters drawn to his pledge to avoid new entanglements overseas. Trump returned to power in 2024 by promising affordability, castigating the Biden Administration over inflation, and appealing to what his aides described as nostalgia for the pre-pandemic economy. As fuel prices and consumer costs climbed, the conflict in Iran threatened to erode some of his central campaign promises.

Trump also faced a Catch-22 of sorts. He wants to end the war, but not without achieving objectives that would definitively prevent Iran from inching closer to a nuclear weapon. In internal discussions, some national-security officials warned a sustained assault might do more to accelerate Tehran's ambitions than deter them. "The only way they will think they can prevent something like this from happening again is to have a nuclear weapon," says another White House official. "There is more of a burden on us now to have a tangible, enforceable agreement that keeps them sufficiently blocked from crossing the nuclear threshold."

As the fighting drags on, Trump has been struck by Tehran's resolve. "They are very tough. They're able to withstand tremendous pain," he tells TIME. "So I respect them for that. The fact is, I think they're better negotiators than they are fighters." The Administration now faces an acrobatic challenge: finding an off-ramp without appearing to have achieved too little. Engineering a successor regime that is more stable and Western-friendly than the one he aims to displace is proving trickier than Trump thought it would. The war has come to resemble a grim game of whack-a-mole, as one Administration official describes it, with strikes eliminating successive leaders as officials search for a viable alternative to emerge from the wreckage.

When Trump spoke to TIME early in March, he talked of regime change: "I want to be involved in the selection," of a new Iranian leader, he said. "They can select, but we have to make sure it's somebody that's reasonable to the United States." Such an outcome is difficult to envision; in his April 1 speech, he falsely claimed it was never the goal. The hope, aides say, is that the degradation of Iran's military capacity and decapitation of its leadership infrastructure will foreclose the possibility of a nuclear-armed state, dismantle its ballistic missile program, and create the conditions for internal change. But that, too, is fraught. Ordinary Iranians are largely unarmed, facing an advanced army willing to deploy overwhelming force against its own people.

Independent analysts say reopening the strait would likely require either a sustained military occupation with U.S. boots on the ground or a negotiated end to hostilities. Neither path is simple. Netanyahu and Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman are inclined to prolong the conflict, viewing it as a rare opportunity to weaken a common adversary. But they also recognize their dependence on Trump's timeline. With Israeli elections looming, Netanyahu has little room to maneuver without Trump's backing, an Israeli official says. "They'll do what I tell them," Trump tells TIME of the Israelis. "They've been a good team player. They'll stop when I stop. They'll stop unless they're provoked, in which case, they'll have no choice, but they'll stop when I stop."

How the war may shape November's elections—and what those results will mean for the rest of his presidency—is a question that hangs over Trump's decisions. Some advisers detect a note of resignation in the President's thinking. In private discussions, he often points out that the party in power tends to lose ground in the midterms. "He's having trouble getting past the history," an aide observes. But history also suggests there can be worse outcomes for a President who takes the nation to war than losing an election.

## 二、中文翻译 (Chinese Translation)

### 白宫内部的"坏消息"

伊朗战争进入第三周，特朗普正在椭圆形办公室里，他最信任的一批顾问前来传递了令人不安的消息。他的长期民调师托尼·法布里齐奥进行的调查显示，特朗普发动的这场战争正变得越来越不受欢迎。油价飙升突破每加仑4美元，股市跌至多年低点，数百万美国人准备走上街头抗议。已确认有13名美国军人阵亡。特朗普的一些关键公众支持者正在批评这场看不到尽头的冲突。白宫幕僚长苏茜·怀尔斯和一小群助手不得不告诉总统：战争拖得越久，就越会威胁到他的民众支持率和共和党在11月中期选举中的前景。

特朗普感到不安。据一名高级政府官员透露，总统近来许多个早晨都在观看军方官员汇编的战场胜利视频。他曾对顾问们说，作为消除伊朗核威胁的总司令，可能成为他最具标志性的成就之一。但据两名白宫消息人士称，怀尔斯担心助手们只给总统呈现"玫瑰色的景象"，只说他想听的话而非他需要听的话。她敦促同事们"对老板更加坦诚"。

### 时间正在流逝

这次会议反映了白宫再也无法忽视的现实：在总统、他的政党和美国公众付出更沉重代价之前，时间正在流逝。特朗普曾承诺重振经济、让美国远离海外冲突。而现在，他发动了一场未经民意授权的战争，经济之痛可能才刚刚开始。进入现代历史上最大石油冲击的一个月后，全球增长预期正在被大幅下调，欧洲和亚洲出现短缺，能源交易商警告世界尚未感受到这场冲击的全部严重性。霍尔木兹海峡——这条波斯湾石油和天然气的主要出口窄水道——若长期关闭，可能将全球经济推入衰退。

面对这一困局，特朗普感到沮丧，与自己的一些官员产生分歧，对战争的负面报道也怒不可遏。据两名顾问和两名与他交谈过的国会议员透露，不断攀升的政治和经济代价让他开始寻找"退出通道"（off-ramp）。特朗普告诉他们，他想结束这场军事行动，对旷日持久的冲突可能拖累共和党中期选举前景感到担忧。但与此同时，他又希望这次行动能取得决定性胜利。盟友们说，他正在寻找一种方式：宣布胜利、停止战斗，并寄希望于经济状况在政治损害固化之前趋于稳定。一位高级政府官员说："窗口期非常狭窄。"

### 4月1日的全国讲话

特朗普在4月1日的黄金时段全国讲话中试图"穿针引线"。他宣扬了军事胜利，称行动"即将完成"，同时又表示美国将在未来两到三周内对伊朗伊斯兰共和国"极其猛烈地"打击，威胁要摧毁其能源基础设施。"我们要把他们打回石器时代，"总统说，"那才是他们该待的地方。"

次日上午在接受TIME电话采访时，特朗普声称伊朗急于达成协议结束战斗。"他们为什么不打电话来？我们昨晚刚炸了他们三座大桥，"总统说。"他们正在被消灭。他们说特朗普没在跟伊朗谈判。我是说，这算是个简单的谈判。"

### 出乎意料的伊朗反击

然而，在强硬言辞的背后，白宫西翼内部越来越认识到局势可能正在失控。包括国防部长皮特·赫格塞斯在内的关键特朗普官员，对德黑兰针对美国和以色列在整个地区目标发动的报复性袭击规模感到震惊——攻击波及了科威特、巴林、沙特阿拉伯、阿联酋和卡塔尔等长期被认为"不会被波及"的国家。卡塔尔既收容过伊朗的恐怖代理人，又充当过美国与哈马斯之间的秘密外交渠道。这一回应打破了德黑兰会将自己局限于"表演性报复"的假设。在战前的内部讨论中，赫格塞斯曾以伊朗对特朗普过去打击的温和反应为据，认为精确校准的武力可以在不引发更广泛战争的情况下对德黑兰施加代价。一位了解其想法的知情人士说，赫格塞斯"措手不及，毫无疑问"。

五角大楼则反驳了这一说法。赫格塞斯的首席发言人肖恩·帕内尔告诉TIME："美军是世界上最先进、最全面、最经受战火考验的规划组织。在'史诗之怒行动'发动之前，我们已经预判、兵棋推演并全面准备了伊朗的每一种可能反应，从最弱的反应到最极端的升级。伊朗做的任何事都不会让我们感到意外。我们已准备就绪，我们占据主导，我们正在获胜。"

### 军事成果与未竟目标

据五角大楼统计，"史诗之怒行动"取得了明确的军事成功：伊朗90%的导弹能力被削弱或摧毁，约70%的发射装置被中和，超过150艘军舰被瘫痪或摧毁，伊朗最高领袖阿里·哈梅内伊以及多名高级副手被击杀。然而，特朗普此前宣扬的更广泛目标——永久阻断德黑兰的核武之路、拆除其弹道导弹项目、以更友好的政权取代伊斯兰共和国的神权强硬派——在白宫设定的压缩时间表内越来越不可能实现。

在讲话中，特朗普将行动描绘为即将胜利。"我们握有所有的牌，他们一张没有，"他说。"我们正按计划即将完成美国的所有军事目标。"但最终结局依然模糊：特朗普同时承诺要加强战斗和结束战斗。他发誓要使用前所未有的手段对伊朗释放毁灭性力量，但又告诉TIME他绝不会允许人工智能做出致命决定，坚持人类将始终控制指挥链。"我不会让AI来做，"特朗普说。"我尊重AI。这是总统必须做的决定——前提是他称职。"除此之外，他似乎不愿将任何选项排除在外。

总统的老友兼特使史蒂夫·威特科夫将此归因于一段商业生涯——在那个世界里，保持选项的灵活性至关重要。"唐纳德·特朗普总是保留多个退出策略，"威特科夫对白宫和国务院的同事们说。"他保留大量选项、大量退出通道，然后凭感觉摸索前进。"但战争有一种超出总统计划的方式。特朗普赌博的风险在于：在未来几周内加强军事行动，可能更多地关闭而非创造退路。

### "一锤定音"策略与内塔尼亚胡的推动

战争准备开始时，政府相信自己有一套制胜方案：美国将发动一次压倒性的开场打击，使德黑兰唯一可行的回应只能是有限报复——足以向国内民众交代，但不至于招来更多攻击。这一理论根植于先例。特朗普在第一任期下令击杀伊朗将军卡西姆·苏莱曼尼时，伊朗的回应是对美军基地发射导弹，未造成人员伤亡且提前做了预告。2025年6月针对伊朗核设施的"午夜之锤行动"之后，报复同样是克制的。

特朗普一直青睐助手们所称的"一次搞定"行动。他在也门、叙利亚和索马里都实施过此类行动。今年1月，他大胆地将委内瑞拉领导人尼古拉斯·马杜罗秘密押送出国在美国受审，为更顺从的合作伙伴——代理总统德尔西·罗德里格斯——的上台创造了空间。随后他推动美国获取委内瑞拉的石油储备——全球最大的石油储备之一。助手们说，特朗普将委内瑞拉视为一个示范：迅速的外科手术式干预可以推翻敌对政权、安装合作性替代者，并在不将国家拖入无限期对抗的情况下确保美国利益。

以色列总理本雅明·内塔尼亚胡——对伊朗军事侵略的拥护者——有不同的想法。在过去六个月中，内塔尼亚胡反复告诉特朗普，过去对伊朗的成功打击应成为更持久的"最终战役"的序幕。2月11日，内塔尼亚胡来到华盛顿与总统进行了长达数小时的私人会谈。据一位在场消息人士称，内塔尼亚胡告诉特朗普："我们已走到这一步了，唐纳德。我们必须完成我们开始的事。"伊朗在拖延时间，内塔尼亚胡告诉特朗普，他们会秘密冲刺制造核弹。另一位以色列官员说："上次被打之后，他们觉得自己没什么好失去的了。"他认为德黑兰会将发展核武器视为防止此类打击再次发生的唯一方法。

### 战前保密与"声东击西"

攻击计划在执行前近一个月就已启动，经过数周精心协调，大部分与以色列方面密切磋商。当纽约时报于2月17日发表行动规划的细节后，据一名高级政府官员称，特朗普对助手们大发雷霆，骂了一连串脏话。总统随后告诉记者他将在"10到15天"内决定是否打击，尽管他知道美国计划更快发动攻击。一位白宫官员说："他是有意进行公开误导以保护任务。"

特朗普对泄密变得极为警惕，以至于他自己的一些助手也成了"声东击西"的对象。2月27日，他前往海湖庄园。助手们在一个临时战情室中集合。特朗普对在场人数之多感到不快。一名官员回忆说："他觉得人太多了"——其中包括他不认识或觉得不够熟悉的人。某一刻，总统突然宣称行动取消，说他还要继续斟酌。这又是一次"虚晃一枪"：特朗普已下定决心当晚就发动进攻。人群散去后，他召回了一个更小的、值得信任的圈子——那些他希望在第一枚炸弹落下时陪在身边的人。

### 海湖庄园星空下的晚宴与万斯的异议

当晚，特朗普在海湖庄园的露台上与一群人共进晚餐，包括副幕僚长斯蒂芬·米勒、国务卿马尔科·卢比奥、威特科夫和白宫法律顾问戴维·沃灵顿。副总统J.D.万斯不在场，他在华盛顿的战情室中。一名特朗普官员表示，这是标准的政府连续性协议的体现——在敏感的国家安全行动期间，当总统和副总统都不在白宫时，要求两人保持分离。据两名了解讨论情况的消息人士透露，在总统的核心圈子中，万斯是最坚定反对此次行动的人。特朗普在当晚棕榈滩星空下对众人说："J.D.真的不喜欢这个。但当决定做出后，就是决定了，对吧？"

白宫消息人士称，万斯在进攻前陈述了他认为的利弊两面，并补充说"一旦总统做出决定，副总统百分之一百一十地支持他"。（万斯的一名助手拒绝置评。）

### "史诗之怒行动"的展开与霍尔木兹海峡冲击

"史诗之怒行动"以击杀伊朗最高领袖的全面打击拉开序幕。德黑兰的回应是全方位的：导弹和无人机攻击驻伊拉克和叙利亚的美军基地、对以色列城市发射火力、骚扰波斯湾商业航运，以及地区代理民兵的协同攻击。了解赫格塞斯想法的知情人士说，赫格塞斯属于措手不及的人之一："他预料到伊朗人会以某种形式反击。当他们开始攻击几乎整个地区时，他就像被震了一下：'哇，我们真的陷进去了。'"

令政府同样措手不及的是，伊朗动用了一张杠杆牌：控制霍尔木兹海峡——全球约20%的石油日供应量经此通过。针对美国的打击，德黑兰实施了事实上的封锁，宣布该航道实际关闭，仅允许非敌对船只通行。由此产生的经济冲击在国内引发的连锁反应超出了特朗普核心圈子的预期。随着油价飙升，特朗普试图将更高的成本重新定义为必要的权衡——为消除核武伊朗威胁而付出的短期代价。

### 支持率与竞选承诺的侵蚀

一方面，特朗普认为伊朗的激烈反应有其价值，相信它会证实他关于伊斯兰共和国构成破坏稳定威胁的论点。特朗普在3月4日的电话中告诉我："既然你们已经看到了他们过去两天对该地区其他国家的行为，他们本会把这些国家全部消灭。"然而，顾问们担心这场战争会疏远那些被他承诺避免新的海外纠葛所吸引的政治支持者。特朗普在2024年回归权力时承诺降低物价、痛斥拜登政府的通胀，并诉诸助手们所描述的"对疫情前经济的怀旧"。随着燃料价格和消费成本攀升，伊朗冲突威胁到了他的一些核心竞选承诺。

### 进退两难的"第二十二条军规"

特朗普面临一种"第二十二条军规"式的困境：他想结束战争，但又不愿在未达成能确实阻止伊朗接近核武器的目标前收手。在内部讨论中，一些国家安全官员警告，持续打击可能反而会加速德黑兰的核雄心而非遏制它们。一名白宫官员说："他们认为唯一能防止此类事件再次发生的办法就是拥有核武器。现在我们有更重的负担，需要达成一个切实可执行的协议，使他们被充分阻隔在核门槛之外。"

随着战事拖延，特朗普对德黑兰的韧性印象深刻。他告诉TIME："他们非常强硬，能承受巨大痛苦。所以我尊重他们。事实是，我认为他们是比战士更好的谈判者。"

### 寻找"退出通道"的挑战

政府如今面临一项"高空走钢丝"式的挑战：找到退出通道，同时又不能显得成果太少。构建一个比他要推翻的政权更稳定、更亲西方的继任政权，比特朗普预想的要困难得多。这场战争变成了一位政府官员所形容的残酷"打地鼠游戏"：打击消灭一个个继任领导人，同时寻找一个可行的替代者从废墟中崛起。

特朗普3月初与TIME交谈时谈到了政权更迭："我想参与挑选，"他谈到新的伊朗领导人时说。"他们可以选，但我们必须确保选出的人对美国来说是合理的。"这样的结果难以想象；在4月1日的讲话中，他谎称这从来不是目标。助手们说，希望在于：伊朗军事能力的削弱和领导层基础设施的"斩首"将排除核武装国家的可能性，拆除其弹道导弹项目，并为内部变革创造条件。但这同样充满风险。普通伊朗人基本没有武装，面对的是一支愿意对本国人民动用压倒性武力的先进军队。

独立分析人士指出，重新开放海峡可能需要美军地面部队的持续军事占领，或者通过谈判结束敌对行动——两条路都不简单。内塔尼亚胡和沙特王储穆罕默德·本·萨勒曼倾向于延长冲突，将其视为削弱共同对手的难得机会，但他们也认识到自己对特朗普时间表的依赖。随着以色列选举临近，一位以色列官员说，没有特朗普的支持，内塔尼亚胡几乎没有回旋余地。特朗普告诉TIME谈到以色列人时："他们会听我的。他们一直是好队友。我停的时候他们就停。除非他们受到挑衅别无选择，否则我停他们就停。"

### 笼罩决策的政治阴影

战争如何影响11月的选举——以及选举结果对其总统余任的意义——这个问题悬在特朗普的每一个决定之上。一些顾问察觉到总统思维中有一丝听天由命的意味。在私下讨论中，他常指出执政党往往在中期选举中失利。一名助手观察到："他难以摆脱历史的阴影。"但历史也表明，对一位将国家带入战争的总统来说，还有比输掉选举更糟糕的结局。

## 三、时髦术语与热词讲解 (Buzzwords & Trend Terms)

### 1. Off-ramp（退出通道/下匝道）

源自高速公路的驶出匝道。在地缘政治和外交语境中，指为冲突中的一方提供一种体面撤退或降级的路径。文章核心词汇——特朗普正在寻找一个能宣布胜利并抽身的"off-ramp"。这个词在俄乌冲突中也被大量使用。

>**例句：** *"The mounting political and economic toll has left him looking for an off-ramp."*

### 2. Operation Epic Fury（史诗之怒行动）

美军对伊朗军事行动的代号。美国军事行动的命名通常追求气势与心理震慑效果，如"沙漠风暴"(Desert Storm)、"午夜之锤"(Midnight Hammer)等，体现了军事传播学中的"叙事塑造"手法。

### 3. Catch-22（第二十二条军规）

源自约瑟夫·海勒同名讽刺小说，指一种逻辑上自相矛盾、进退两难的困境。文章中用来描述特朗普"想结束战争但又不能不实现目标"的窘境。此词已成为英语中表达"两难悖论"的经典表达。

> **例句：** *"Trump also faced a Catch-22 of sorts."*

### 4. Performative retaliation（表演性报复）

指一种以展示决心为目的、实际损害有限的军事回应，本质上是做给国内观众看的。文章指出美方此前预期伊朗会采取此类"表演性"反击，结果判断失误。这个术语融合了国际关系中的"信号博弈"(signaling game)理论。

> **例句：** *"The response shattered the assumption that Tehran would confine itself to performative retaliation."*

### 5. Decapitation（斩首行动）

军事术语，指通过消灭敌方最高领导层来瘫痪其指挥体系。文章中"decapitation of its leadership infrastructure"意为系统性消灭伊朗领导层。

### 6. One-and-done（一锤定音/一次搞定）

源自体育用语（打一场就走），这里指特朗普偏好的快速、有限军事干预模式——快速打击、达成目标、迅速撤离，避免陷入长期纠缠。

> **例句：** *"Trump has long favored what aides call 'one-and-done' operations."*

### 7. Rose-colored view（玫瑰色的视角）

即"戴着玫瑰色眼镜看世界"，指过于乐观、美化现实的观点。怀尔斯担心助手们给特朗普呈现的就是这种粉饰太平的信息。

> **例句：** *"Wiles was concerned aides were giving the President a rose-colored view."*

### 8. Whack-a-mole（打地鼠）

源自街机游戏，在军事/政策语境中指一种"消灭一个冒出另一个"的低效循环。文章用它来形容美军在消灭一个个伊朗领导人后无法建立稳定继任体制的困局。

> **例句：** *"The war has come to resemble a grim game of whack-a-mole."*

### 9. Nuclear threshold（核门槛）

指一个国家距离获得可用核武器的技术和政治临界点。"crossing the nuclear threshold"意味着跨越这一门槛正式成为核国家。

> **例句：** *"...keeps them sufficiently blocked from crossing the nuclear threshold."*

### 10. Thread the needle（穿针引线）

比喻在极窄的空间内精确操作，引申为在多重矛盾约束中找到一条极其微妙的平衡路径。特朗普在全国讲话中试图同时安抚鹰派和民众情绪，正是在"穿针引线"。

> **例句：** *"Trump tried to thread the needle in a primetime address to the nation."*

### 11. Head fake（虚晃一枪）

源自篮球/橄榄球中的假动作，指故意释放虚假信息以误导对手。特朗普宣称取消行动实际上是"head fake"，真正目的是保密。

> **例句：** *"This was another head fake: Trump had already made up his mind to attack that very night."*

### 12. Continuity-of-government protocol（政府连续性协议）

国家安全中的标准程序，在敏感军事行动期间要求总统和副总统不在同一地点，以确保紧急情况下政府指挥链不中断。

### 13. De facto blockade（事实封锁）

"De facto"为拉丁语，意为"事实上的"，与"de jure"（法律上的）相对。指虽未经正式宣战或法律程序，但在实际效果上构成封锁的行为。

> **例句：** *"Tehran implemented a de facto blockade, declaring the channel effectively closed."*

### 14. Optionality（选项灵活性）

来自商业/金融术语，指保留尽可能多的选择余地而不过早承诺。威特科夫用这个概念解释特朗普的决策风格。

> **例句：** *"...a business career in which maintaining optionality was paramount."*

### 15. Midterms（中期选举）

指美国总统任期中间举行的国会选举，通常被视为对现任总统执政表现的"公投"。历史上执政党几乎总是在中期选举中失去席位。

### 16. Subterfuge（瞒天过海/暗中欺骗）

源自拉丁语 subterfugere（秘密逃避），指通过欺骗手段达到目的的行为。文章中描述特朗普对泄密极度警惕，以至于连自己的幕僚都成了被欺骗的对象——他在海湖庄园战情室中假装取消行动，实际上当晚就发动了进攻。这个词揭示了战时决策中信任的脆弱性：当总统连自己人都不信任时，决策圈的萎缩本身就是一种风险。

> **例句：** *"Trump became wary enough of leaks that some of his own aides were the target of subterfuge."*

## 四、长难句解析 (Complex Sentence Analysis)

### 句1

> **原文：** *"The mounting political and economic toll has left him looking for an off-ramp, according to two advisers and two members of Congress who have spoken to him during the last week."*

**结构拆解：**

- 主语：The mounting political and economic toll（不断攀升的政治和经济代价）
- 谓语：has left him looking for（使他不得不寻找）— "leave sb. doing sth." 结构表示"使某人处于……的状态"
- 宾语：an off-ramp（一条退路）
- 状语从句：according to two advisers and two members of Congress who have spoken to him during the last week（信息来源插入语）
- "mounting" 是现在分词作定语，表示"不断积累/攀升的"

**翻译：** 据过去一周内与他交谈过的两名顾问和两名国会议员透露，不断攀升的政治和经济代价已使他不得不开始寻找一条退路。

---

### 句2

>**原文：** *"In internal deliberations before the war's launch, Hegseth had pointed to Iran's muted reaction to Trump's past attacks as evidence that calibrated force could impose costs on Tehran without triggering a broader war."*

**结构拆解：**

- 状语：In internal deliberations before the war's launch（在战争发动前的内部讨论中）
- 主语：Hegseth
- 谓语：had pointed to（曾指出）— 过去完成时，表示战争发动之前的动作
- 宾语：Iran's muted reaction to Trump's past attacks（伊朗对特朗普以往打击的温和反应）
- 补语：as evidence that...（作为……的证据）
- that 从句：calibrated force could impose costs on Tehran without triggering a broader war

**关键词：**

- muted reaction：温和/克制的反应
- calibrated force：精确校准的武力
- impose costs on：对……施加代价

**翻译：** 在战争发动前的内部讨论中，赫格塞斯曾指出伊朗对特朗普以往打击的温和反应，作为"精确校准的武力可以在不引发更大规模战争的情况下对德黑兰施加代价"这一论点的佐证。

---

### 句3

>**原文：** *"A prolonged closure of the Strait of Hormuz, the narrow waterway that is the primary exit route for oil and gas from the Persian Gulf, could tip the global economy into recession."*

**结构拆解：**

- 主语：A prolonged closure of the Strait of Hormuz（霍尔木兹海峡的长期关闭）
- 同位语：the narrow waterway that is the primary exit route for oil and gas from the Persian Gulf（解释霍尔木兹海峡是什么）
- 谓语：could tip（可能将……推入）
- 宾语+补语：the global economy into recession（全球经济陷入衰退）
- "tip...into..." 是一个生动的比喻，如同将天平推向一侧

**翻译：** 霍尔木兹海峡——这条波斯湾石油和天然气的主要出口窄水道——若长期关闭，可能将全球经济推入衰退。

---

### 句4

>**原文：** *"He has told advisers that being the commander in chief to eliminate the nuclear threat posed by Iran could be one of his signature achievements."*

**结构拆解：**

- 主句：He has told advisers that...
- that 从句中：
  - 主语：动名词短语 "being the commander in chief to eliminate the nuclear threat posed by Iran"（作为消除伊朗核威胁的总司令）
  - 谓语：could be
  - 表语：one of his signature achievements（他的标志性成就之一）
- "posed by Iran" 是过去分词短语作后置定语修饰 "nuclear threat"
- "signature achievements" 意为"标志性/代表性成就"

**翻译：** 他曾告诉顾问们，作为消除伊朗所构成核威胁的三军统帅，可能成为他最具标志性的成就之一。

---

### 句5

>**原文：** *"The Administration now faces an acrobatic challenge: finding an off-ramp without appearing to have achieved too little."*

**结构拆解：**

- 主语：The Administration
- 谓语：faces
- 宾语：an acrobatic challenge（一项杂技式的挑战）— "acrobatic" 用得极妙，暗示高难度、需要精确平衡
- 冒号后为同位语解释：
  - finding an off-ramp（找到退出通道）
  - without appearing to have achieved too little（又不能显得成果太少）
  - "appearing to have achieved" 是动名词+不定式完成体的复合结构

**翻译：** 政府如今面临一项"高空走钢丝"式的挑战：找到一条退出通道，同时又不能显得收获寥寥。

---

### 句6

>**原文：** *"Engineering a successor regime that is more stable and Western-friendly than the one he aims to displace is proving trickier than Trump thought it would."*

**结构拆解：**

- 主语：动名词短语 "Engineering a successor regime that is more stable and Western-friendly than the one he aims to displace"
  - Engineering：构建/打造
  - that 定语从句修饰 regime
  - the one = the regime; he aims to displace = 他意图取代的
- 谓语：is proving（正被证明）— "prove" 作系动词
- 表语：trickier than Trump thought it would（比特朗普预想的更棘手）
  - "it would" 省略了 "be"

**翻译：** 构建一个比他意图取代的政权更稳定、更亲西方的继任政权，正被证明比特朗普预想的要棘手得多。

---

### 句7

>**原文：** *"But wars have a way of outrunning a President's plans. The risk of Trump's gamble is that intensifying the military campaign in the coming weeks will do more to close exit routes than to create them."*

**结构拆解：**

- 第一句："have a way of doing sth." 是固定搭配，意为"总是会……/有……的倾向"
  - outrunning = 跑在前面/超出
- 第二句：
  - 主语：The risk of Trump's gamble（特朗普赌博的风险）
  - 表语从句：that intensifying the military campaign... will do more to close exit routes than to create them
  - "do more to... than to..." 比较结构：更多地关闭而非创造退路

**翻译：** 但战争总有超出总统计划的方式。特朗普赌博的风险在于：在未来几周内加强军事行动，可能更多地关闭而非创造退路。

---

### 句8

>**原文：** *"Trump returned to power in 2024 by promising affordability, castigating the Biden Administration over inflation, and appealing to what his aides described as nostalgia for the pre-pandemic economy."*

**结构拆解：**

- 主句：Trump returned to power in 2024
- 方式状语（三个并列动名词短语）：
  1. by promising affordability（承诺降低物价）
  2. castigating the Biden Administration over inflation（严厉批评拜登政府的通胀问题）
  3. appealing to what his aides described as nostalgia for the pre-pandemic economy（诉诸助手们所描述的"对疫情前经济的怀旧"）
- "what his aides described as" 是一个嵌套的宾语从句
- castigate = 严厉批评/痛斥

**翻译：** 特朗普在2024年回归权力，靠的是承诺降低物价、痛斥拜登政府的通胀问题，以及诉诸助手们所描述的"对疫情前经济的怀旧情绪"。

### 句9

>**原文：** *"But history also suggests there can be worse outcomes for a President who takes the nation to war than losing an election."*

**结构拆解：**

- 主语：history
- 谓语：suggests
- 宾语从句：there can be worse outcomes... than losing an election
- "for a President who takes the nation to war" 是介词短语作定语修饰 outcomes
- 比较结构：worse outcomes... than losing an election — "比输掉选举更糟糕的结局"
- 这里使用了 **litotes（低调陈述法/含蓄否定修辞）** ——不说"更糟糕的结局是什么"，让读者自行填充（弹劾？历史审判？身败名裂？），留白的力量远大于明说

**修辞分析：** 这是全文的落锤之句。前一句说特朗普担心中期选举失利，读者以为这就是最大的风险。紧接着作者轻描淡写地补上这一句——"但历史也表明，还有比输掉选举更糟糕的结局"——不动声色却余味悠长。它让人想起约翰逊因越战放弃连任、尼克松因水门事件辞职、小布什因伊拉克战争声誉扫地。短短一句话，压缩了半个世纪的历史教训。

**翻译：** 但历史也表明，对一位将国家带入战争的总统来说，还有比输掉选举更糟糕的结局。

## 五、思想赏析 (Thought Analysis)

### 1. "帝国过度延伸"的经典叙事

这篇文章堪称一篇教科书级的政治深度报道，其核心命题触及了国际政治中的一个永恒主题：**大国在发动战争后如何体面收场**。从越战到伊拉克战争，从阿富汗到如今的伊朗，美国反复陷入同一个剧本——以"压倒性武力"开局，却发现军事胜利无法自动转化为政治成果。文章巧妙地展示了这一结构性困境的再次上演。

### 2. "商人思维"与战争逻辑的根本冲突

文章通过史蒂夫·威特科夫的话——"特朗普总是保留多个退出策略……然后凭感觉摸索前进"——揭示了一个深刻的张力：商业世界的"保持灵活性"哲学与战争的不可逆本质之间存在根本性冲突。在房地产交易中，你可以随时退出谈判桌；但在战争中，每一次升级都会关闭而非打开退路。作者敏锐地指出："wars have a way of outrunning a President's plans"（战争总会超出总统的计划）——这句话简洁而沉重，道出了战争的本质规律。

### 3. 信息茧房与权力决策

文章揭示了一个极具现实意义的权力运作病理：**信息过滤效应**。怀尔斯担心助手们给总统呈现"玫瑰色的景象"，这不仅是白宫内部管理问题，更是所有集权式决策结构的通病。当权力核心的信息获取被层层过滤和美化，决策者就会在"胜利视频"的回音室中丧失对现实的判断力。这一幕让人想起越战时期"五点钟愚蠢会议"(Five O'Clock Follies)的历史回声。

### 4. "表演性报复"假设的破产

文章最引人深思的地方之一，是它揭示了美国战略评估中一个致命的认知偏差：**基于过去行为预测未来反应的线性思维**。赫格塞斯以伊朗对此前打击的"温和反应"为据，认为更大规模的打击也只会引来"表演性报复"。这种推理忽略了一个基本事实——当生存受到威胁时，行为模式会发生质变。这恰恰是博弈论中"承诺问题"(commitment problem)的典型案例：过去的克制并不意味着永远的克制。

### 5. "第二十二条军规"的核扩散悖论

文章揭示的最深层悖论在于：**试图通过军事手段消除核威胁，反而可能加速核扩散** 。一位白宫官员清醒地指出——德黑兰如今比以往任何时候都更相信只有核武器才能保护自己不再遭受类似打击。这正是国际关系学者长期警告的"安全困境"(security dilemma)的经典表现：你越是通过武力来消除威胁，对方就越有动力获取终极威慑手段。

### 6. 战争的民主成本

文章隐含着一个深层追问：民主国家发动战争的政治约束机制是否有效？特朗普发动了一场"未经民意授权"的战争，而民意的反弹似乎更多来自经济痛苦（油价、股市）而非对战争本身正义性的质疑。这揭示了一个令人不安的现实——在现代民主政治中，经济利益往往比道德判断更能约束战争行为。

### 7. 新闻写作的匠心

从写作技法来看，作者Eric Cortellessa展现了出色的叙事功力。文章以一个戏剧性场景开篇——幕僚向总统传递坏消息——立即建立了紧张感。全文在宏观战略分析与微观人物细节之间自如切换：海湖庄园星空下的晚宴、特朗普对在场陌生面孔的不悦、万斯的沉默反对……这些细节让地缘政治叙事有了血肉和温度。结尾的那句——"but history also suggests there can be worse outcomes for a President who takes the nation to war than losing an election"——不动声色却余味悠长，是一记冷峻的历史警钟。

### 8. 匿名信源的政治学：泄密本身就是权力斗争

这篇文章的信源结构本身就值得作为一个独立的分析对象。全文大量使用匿名信源——"a senior Administration official"、"two White House sources"、"a person familiar with his thinking"、"two sources familiar with the deliberations"——几乎每一个关键细节都来自不愿具名的内部人士。这不是记者的懒惰，而是深度政治报道的标准范式。但更值得追问的是：这些人为什么要说？

在任何白宫中，向媒体泄露信息从来不是无目的的行为。怀尔斯担心信息过滤的细节被透露出来，本身就可能是怀尔斯阵营希望通过媒体向总统传递"你需要听真话"的信号。万斯反对战争的细节被披露，可能是万斯团队在为其未来的政治前途铺路——无论战争结果如何，万斯都可以说"我当初是反对的"。赫格塞斯"措手不及"的消息被泄露，可能来自五角大楼内部对其判断力不满的人。

换言之，我们读到的每一条匿名信息，背后都有一个政治意图。泄密不是权力运作的副产品，而是权力斗争的一种形式。作者让读者看到了决策的内幕，但聪明的读者也应该意识到：这些"内幕"本身经过了信源的筛选和塑造。这正是高质量政治新闻的双重阅读层次——你既在读事件本身，也在读信息是如何被释放的。
